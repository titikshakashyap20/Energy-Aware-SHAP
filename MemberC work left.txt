## Task 1 — Statistical Significance: Learned Scheduler vs. Joblib

**Priority: Highest**

Determine whether the learned scheduler performs meaningfully differently from the Joblib baseline using the existing nested Leave-One-Workload-Out (LOWO) evaluation.

### Requirements

1. Load `results/memberC/scheduler_nested_evaluation.csv` and inspect the evaluation protocol and relevant scripts before calculating any statistics.
2. Identify the correct regret metric, its definition, the workload identifiers and how scheduler and Joblib results are paired.
3. Aggregate the results to obtain one comparable regret value per workload and method, giving 18 paired workload-level observations if all 18 workloads have valid pairs.
4. Define the difference consistently as:

   `difference = scheduler regret - Joblib regret`

   Positive differences indicate higher regret for the scheduler; negative differences indicate lower regret.
5. Compute the mean paired difference across workloads.
6. Run a paired bootstrap with 10,000 resamples, resampling the 18 workloads as paired units. Calculate and report a 95% confidence interval for the mean difference. Use a fixed random seed and document the interval method.
7. Run a two-sided Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the 18 paired workload-level differences. Report the test statistic, p-value, sample size and the handling of zero differences and ties.
8. Report the mean regret of each method alongside the mean paired difference, confidence interval and p-value.
9. Save the workload-level paired data and a machine-readable statistical summary.

### Critical statistical requirements

- **Resample by workload, not by individual decision point.** The 72 scheduler decision points are not 72 independent workloads; observations within a workload may be correlated.
- Use the same evaluation protocol for both methods.
- Do not treat the 18 LOWO folds as 18 independent retrainings of the entire development process. Explain the limits of inference from this evaluation design.
- Do not interpret a non-significant p-value as proof of equivalence.
- Do not claim that the scheduler outperforms Joblib unless the statistical results support that conclusion.
- Keep the paired workload-level values available for auditing and reproduction.

### Expected outputs

- `results/memberC/scheduler_joblib_significance.csv`
- `results/memberC/scheduler_joblib_paired_differences.csv`
- A concise report containing the mean difference, 95% confidence interval, Wilcoxon statistic, p-value and interpretation.
